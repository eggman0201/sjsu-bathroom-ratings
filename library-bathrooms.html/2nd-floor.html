MLK, Jr. Library: 2nd Floor Female Bathroom

Back to Home

Current Rating:
★1/2

Full Review:

Hoo, where to start with this one. I have reviewed many bathrooms. I have a set of principles I stick to in the name of accessibility and dignified bathroom experiences. I have learned to anticipate all sorts of different flaws, like nonfunctioning features, accessibility oversights, and the occasional bathroom mess. I have also forgotten that these things are used by the public and to never assume I can expect all things.

For starters, there were several signs near the bathroom all leading to it, which was extremely strange because none of the other bathrooms have this. I guess because it's on the other side of the floor as the bathrooms above it? I have gotten lost trying to find this bathroom in the past though, so maybe this is an appropriate measure. As always, the Braille signage at the entrance was correct. As I entered, I saw a flashing fire alarm. Nice.

This bathroom was really small and cramped. For such a popular floor (if I had to guess, this is the MOST popular floor for students and the public), the bathroom only had 3 stalls, including the accessible stall. This would be a tight squeeze for anyone, let alone anyone with mobility aids. It wasn't terribly dirty upon entering. There was a flashing fire alarm, which is always nice to see. The stall's toiletries were stocked, but it was an extremely tight squeeze. Out of all the bathrooms I've reviewed, I have struggled to turn around in this one the most. I typically have a large messenger bag I carry, and while that obviously erases a lot of the space I have to move, this was a bathroom I legitimately felt claustrophobic in. The lock also literally did not work at all. I tried it at different states of closed-ness, and not a single angle worked, making this the first time I was glad a door was hard to open and close. Luckily, the actual bathroom experience was pretty typical; the toilet, as always, was loud (though, the stall being as small as it was made the experience more unpleasant than normal), and there was a bag hook but no platform.

Someone had gone around placing stickers around the bathroom. I'm not sure how others feel about impromptu bathroom decorators, but the stickers were pretty cute and made me feel better about being in such a cramped space.

As I washed my hands, both soap dispensers worked, and the one air dryer I used (didn't notice the other one) worked just fine. The pad and tampon dispensers worked.

Now, if you've seen my other reviews, you're probably wondering why I haven't said anything about the accessible stall. I'll start off by stating the obvious: there was no emergency call button (on why this matters to me, check out one of the links at the bottom of the page). That's not why I left this for last. For whatever reason, the baby changing station was left open, and what I can only assume were weed crumbs were left on it. I didn't smell weed as I entered, but upon leaving and reentering, I could swear I was able to smell it, though my sense of smell isn't exactly acute. I thought I must have misjudged, and maybe I have. For this reason, I've taken some close-ups so that someone can correct me if I'm wrong.

I understand that this particular bathroom is open for the public and is subject to the public happening to it. However, it is really disappointing to see a bathroom stall reserved for vulnerable people, whether they're disabled or need a private space to change their kids, be desecrated like this. I can only hope it wasn't a student smoking up in the bathroom because that's really lame and embarrassing. I would also presume that it could be a health hazard if someone did put a kid on the station. I don't know, that's just a really douchy thing to do in a public restroom.

Overall, even with the weed thing aside, I didn't like this bathroom. It wasn't inherently egregious, but there were just enough mildly unpleasant things going for it to turn me off from using it again. It's very death-by-a-thousand-cuts.
