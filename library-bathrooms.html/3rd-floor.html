Full Review:

It's a perfectly fine bathroom. The Braille signage on the entrance is correct, which is cool, though the accessible bathroom doesn't have an emergency call system (on why this could benefit people, check one of the links at the bottom of the page). There's also a baby changing station, which is cool. As a more popular floor, the bathroom's a bit dirty with bits of toilet paper and towels on the ground, but that's par for the course for a public bathroom.

The typical toiletries were stocked, and the bag platform can support the weight of a heavy bag. There's also a bag hook in case you prefer inferior options. The flush is loud but not very powerful; there's always a bit of paper left behind, so you have to flush it twice to get the bowl clean.

This is the bathroom with the fifth and largest crater sink. Having learned that it's an art installation and the point of it, I've come to begrudgingly respect it, but I still don't like it very much. The soap dispensers worked just fine, but none of the air dryers worked. I was a bit surprised by this because I had assumed that the lower floors' air dryers were maintained more often, but I don't really like air dryers, so this doesn't really bother me.

There was nothing of note from this bathroom. It's fine.
